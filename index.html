Key distinction

A wallet may act as an interface or credential store. A tokenized card transaction in a supported wallet generally uses the underlying card payment infrastructure rather than becoming a separate payment rail.

Key operating principle

An unknown or timed-out status is not proof of failure. Query and reconcile the transaction before retrying to reduce duplicate-charge risk.

Research scope

This is a learning project based on public sources, not integration, legal, compliance, certification, or provider-selection advice.

3 · Failure handling playbook

Example operational responses to common payment exceptions. The correct procedure depends on the rail, participant, provider and applicable rules.

Timeout or final status unknown

Action: Check the authoritative provider status, available API or dashboard data, webhook events, and reconciliation records using the relevant transaction identifiers.

Avoid: Creating a fresh charge immediately after a timeout without checking the original transaction.

Capture: Payment ID, order ID, timestamps and timezone, response-code category, event IDs, and relevant idempotency information.

UPI debit, but merchant credit is not confirmed

Action: Check the current transaction status and relevant bank or payment reference. Follow the applicable process and escalation route for that transaction category.

Avoid: Treating a debit as sufficient evidence that the merchant order was paid, or promising a generic reversal timeline.

Payment succeeded, but the order remains unpaid

Action: Match the order and payment identifiers, verify provider events, and correct the order state only after confirming the authoritative payment status.

Avoid: Relying exclusively on a browser redirect or treating repeated event notifications as separate payments.

Refund initiated but not yet credited

Action: Record the refund identifier and timestamp, check the provider status, and communicate the appropriate next step.

Avoid: Promising immediate credit in all cases. Refund initiation and customer-side posting are different events.

Wallet token provisioning or device error

Action: Check supported card, device and regional eligibility and capture the non-sensitive error details needed for escalation.

Avoid: Asking customers to send or storing a full card number, CVV, OTP or UPI PIN in a support ticket.

Refund, reversal and chargeback are different

Authorization reversal or void: A supported action associated with reversing an authorization or hold.

Refund: Returning funds in connection with a completed transaction.

Failed-transaction reversal: A process governed by the relevant payment rail's rules and transaction circumstances.

Chargeback or dispute: A separate formal dispute process with its own evidence and deadlines.

4 · Operational tracking and KPI ideas

Authorization success rate Define approved transactions against eligible attempts, then segment by response-code category.
Unknown-status aging Count and age transactions that lack a definitive confirmed status.
Reversal aging Track applicable failed transactions until a reversal or other final outcome is confirmed.
Refund turnaround Distinguish provider refund status from customer-side posting where information is available.
Reconciliation breaks Track unmatched transactions, captures, settlements and payouts.
Partner response SLA Record first response, resolution time, escalation owner and follow-up dates.

Suggested exception-tracking fields

Order ID, payment ID, payment rail, provider, amount and currency, timestamp and timezone, transaction status, response-code category, relevant bank or provider reference, event timestamps, assigned owner, escalation date and final resolution.

Store only the information necessary for investigating the issue. Never include authentication secrets.
